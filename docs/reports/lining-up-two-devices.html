What was measured

Two virtual analysers (logica-vla) on loopback ports, captured together into one capture by the engine's merger (crates/engine/src/group.rs), each setting twice: lined up by a clock both devices see, and lined up at their starts. The shared clock is the analysers' shared signal: 100 Hz, following the computer's clock, so both see the same edges, each at its own samples. Device A's clock is the capture's. Device B starts later (it waits before starting, as a slower device would) and its sample clock runs fast by a set number of ppm. Both sample at 4 MHz, so one sample is 250 ns.

The truth comes from the analysers: each says where its sample 0 is on the computer's clock, so every rising edge of the shared clock belongs to a known period, on either device. The error of a period is where B's edge of that period landed in the capture, minus where A's did. Zero means the two devices agree on when it happened.

Over a 10-second capture

B started about 2 ms after A. Lined up at their starts, B's events land early by that gap from the first sample, and its fast clock moves them by its drift as the capture runs: 500 ppm is half a millisecond a second. Lined up by the shared clock, every one of the 4,000 periods lands on the same sample on both devices, whatever the drift.

At the start of the capture

40 short captures each way, B asked to start 0 to 3.9 ms after A, its clock 50 ppm fast. Lined up at their starts, the error at the start is the start gap, which no one chooses: here B started anywhere from 0.14 ms before A to 4.35 ms after it. Lined up by the shared clock, the error is zero, except where B started more than half a clock period (5 ms) late: a steady clock looks the same one period later, and the merger takes the smallest shift that matches, so it lines B up by the wrong period.

What this means

  • Lined up by a shared signal, two devices agree to the sample, from the first edge to the last, at any drift tried (0 to 500 ppm; crystals are typically within 50). The drift itself is measured on the way and reported.
  • Lined up at their starts, they disagree by the start gap plus the drift. Here that was up to 2.65 ms. At 4 MHz a millisecond is 4,000 samples, so a bus transfer on one device cannot be lined up with its cause on the other, and the gap differs from capture to capture.
  • A steady shared clock is ambiguous by whole periods. In 1 of 40 captures B started more than 5 ms late (asked for under 4 ms) and was lined up 4 periods off: exact within the period, and wrong. An earlier run of the same measurement had 2 of 12. The large gaps came in steps near 30 to 40 ms, which looks like a network acknowledgement delay on the analysers' connection; that is a guess, not measured.

What would fix the ambiguity, not built yet: use the measured moment each device began (the merger sees it to about a millisecond) to choose the period instead of the smallest shift; start devices' connections without send delays (TCP_NODELAY on the virtual analyser's connection); or line up by an irregular shared signal (a data line), which has no repeating period. Until then, a slower shared clock tolerates more: at 10 Hz a start gap up to 50 ms is safe.

Limits of this measurement

  • Virtual analysers, not hardware: their edges are exact to the sample, and their drift is constant. Real devices add edge jitter and drift that wanders with temperature; the straight-line fit follows the average.
  • Both analysers ran on one computer and read one clock, so "the truth" is exact here. With hardware the truth would need a reference instrument.
  • Rising edges of the shared clock only; the result between them follows from the same straight line and was not measured edge by edge.

Reproduce

cargo run --release -p logica-engine --example lineup_report -- lineup.json then node tools/reports/render-lineup.mjs lineup.json report.html. The start gaps are whatever the computer gives, so the slip count varies from run to run.
